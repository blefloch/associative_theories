## Unipotent commutative semigroups that are almost Boolean groups

This conjunction describes commutative semigroups that are close to being Boolean groups.  They are centrally unipotent, namely all squares are equal to the same element `∞` that commutes with every element.  The coset `B = {∞◇x, x∈M}` is a Boolean group with `∞` as its unit element, and the operation on the whole semigroup is given by `x◇y = (∞◇x)◇(∞◇y) ∈ B ⊂ M`.

Such semigroups are obtained from a Boolean group `B` by duplicating some elements.  In detail, `M = A ⊔ B` with `A ⊂ B` and a map `C : M → B` defined as the inclusion map `A ⊂ B` and the identity on `B`, and the product is defined from the operation on `B` by `x ◇ y = C(x) ◇ C(y)`.

The free semigroup on some set `Σ` of generators for this law has `A = Σ` (in the notation above) and `B = 𝒫(Σ)` is the set of finite subsets of `Σ` equipped with the symmetric difference operation.

This conjunction is equivalent to its dual.
