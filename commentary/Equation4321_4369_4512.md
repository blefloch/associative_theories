This conjunction implies that any product of four or more variables can be freely reordered and any power of `x` higher than four is equal to the fourth power.

The free semigroup on some set `Σ` of generators for this law is `Σ ⊔ (Σ×Σ) ⊔ (Σ×Σ×Σ) ⊔ Q(Σ)` modulo an equivalence relation.  Here, `Q(Σ) ⊂ {0,1} × 𝒫(Σ)` is given by `Q(Σ) = ({0} × 𝒫₄(Σ)) ⊔ ({1} × 𝒫₁(Σ))` where `𝒫₄(Σ)` (resp. `𝒫₁(Σ)`) denote finite subsets of `Σ` with at least 4 (resp. 1) elements.  The equivalence relation identifies `(a,b,c) ~ (c,b,a)` and `(a,b,a) ~ (b,a,b)` in `Σ×Σ×Σ`.  The operation has `a ◇ b = (a,b)` and `a ◇ (b,c) = (a,b) ◇ c = (a,b,c)` and all other products are `(m,A)` with `A` the set of all letters appearing in the two operands and `m=1` whenever any such letter is duplicated or whenever one of the operands is `(1,_) ∈ Q(Σ)`.  For instance, `a ◇ (b,c,d) = (0, {a,b,c,d})` if `a,b,c,d` are distinct, and is otherwise `(1, {a,b,c,d})` where `{a,b,c,d}` has between 1 and 3 elements.
